## One-sentence proposition

Living Routes turns a bus ride into a living cultural archive: source-grounded stories explain the place, authorised local voices add memory, and each traveller's own photos or reflections shape what they hear next.

## Why now

Three capabilities have become practical at the same time:

1. A short phone recording can be transcribed, cleaned up, translated and captioned without a studio microphone.
2. A single reviewed source pack can be adapted into short, medium and long versions for a moving journey.
3. Photos, text and voice notes can be turned into a private travel memory while keeping publication opt-in.

GenAI lowers the cost of preparing and maintaining multiple language and duration variants. It does not replace consent, source checking or human review.

## Content supply and quality answer

We do not depend on elderly residents discovering and operating a new app. The cold-start archive uses public heritage material. Local voices can arrive through a WhatsApp voice note, a grandchild, student volunteer or community-centre worker acting as a recording proxy. Ordinary phone audio is enough for a first recording.

Every submission is split into two review modes:

- A personal memory is labelled as a memory, not treated as an objective fact. AI checks safety, privacy and sensitive claims; low-confidence or sensitive items go to a human queue.
- A historical claim is checked against cited sources before it enters the Heritage Facts library.

The demo keeps unauthorised or unreviewed content private. A public story requires explicit consent and human approval.

## Commercial model

We would start B2B/B2G rather than depend on consumer advertising:

- Route or district licensing for bus operators, tourism bodies, museums, hotels and cultural venues.
- Sponsored heritage stops or local-business partnerships, with clear editorial labelling and no paid alteration of historical facts.
- School and university packages for local-history, civic-education and service-learning activities.
- Grants and CSR support for oral-history collection, accessibility and ageing/community participation.

The consumer version can remain free for a small set of routes, with optional paid themed routes or premium memory exports. The operating principle is that sponsorship pays for collection and maintenance; it does not buy the truth of a story.

## Michael's four questions — 30-second answers

### 1. What exists, and why will this work?

There are excellent self-guided walking tours, museum apps and oral-history archives, but they usually optimise for a destination or a fixed exhibition. Living Routes is designed for the minutes people already spend on a bus. We combine verified heritage facts, authorised local voices and a private memory loop, then adapt language and length to the remaining ride. GenAI makes the variants affordable to maintain; human review protects trust.

### 2. How will older people contribute if they do not use the app?

They do not need to learn the app. A WhatsApp voice note or an ordinary phone recording is enough. A grandchild, student volunteer or community-centre worker can act as a recording proxy. The team can run a two-hour recording session at a centre and let AI handle transcription, noise reduction, translation and first-pass tagging. We only publish with consent, and the original voice remains the valuable part.

### 3. How is it economically sustainable?

The first customers are organisations that already pay for cultural interpretation: bus operators, tourism bodies, museums, hotels and schools. They can license a route or district. Local businesses can sponsor a stop with transparent labelling, while grants and CSR budgets can fund oral-history collection. A free consumer layer helps discovery, but the core economics are institutional licensing and funded community collection.

### 4. How do you vet local submissions?

We do not ask AI to decide that a personal memory is a historical fact. Memories are labelled as memories and screened for privacy, safety and harmful claims. Facts must match cited sources. AI handles the majority of routine checks and sends low-confidence or sensitive items to a human review queue. The MVP makes that queue visible instead of hiding it.

## What to show in the demo

1. Same stop, two interests: architecture gets a visual detail; local-life gets a neighbourhood angle.
2. Same stop, two time budgets: the selector chooses short or medium content.
3. One real authorised Cantonese voice note, with transcript, original playback and three-language captions.
4. A private photo/text memory generates a journal entry and changes the next recommendation.
